An integral $I(\hbar)=\int_\Gamma \mathrm{e}^{-S(x)/\hbar}\,dx$ for a polynomial $S$, along a path $\Gamma$ running to infinity where the integrand decays, is a sum of integrals over Lefschetz thimbles, the paths of steepest descent from the critical points of $S$. Each thimble integral has a divergent asymptotic series in $\hbar$. As the phase of $\hbar$ turns, we see the Stokes phenomenon: the thimbles jump, and so does the asymptotic expansion of $I(\hbar)$.

Thimbles

Let $S$ be a polynomial of degree $n+1$, with critical points $p_1,\dots,p_n$, drawn as numbered points above, and let $\hbar\ne0$ be a complex number. The integrand $\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}$ dies in the $n+1$ sectors at infinity where $\mathrm{Re}(S/\hbar)\to+\infty$, the valleys: the white regions of the picture. The integral

$$I(\hbar)=\int_\Gamma\mathrm{e}^{-S(x)/\hbar}\,dx$$

converges for a path $\Gamma$ that comes in along one valley and goes out along another, and by Cauchy’s theorem it depends only on the two valleys. The thimble $J_i$ of $p_i$ is the path through $p_i$ whose points $x$ have

$$S(x)=S(p_i)+a\hbar\quad\text{for some }a\ge0.$$

Along it the phase of the integrand is constant and its modulus falls as steeply as it can, and $S$ maps it two to one onto the ray $S(p_i)+\hbar\,[0,\infty)$ [@witten2011].

The upward flow of $\mathrm{Re}(S/\hbar)$,

$$\frac{dx}{dt}=\overline{S'(x)/\hbar},$$

keeps $\mathrm{Im}(S/\hbar)$ constant, and the Flow slider moves $\Gamma$ along it. It carries every point of $\Gamma$ out to a valley, except the points on the ascending paths $K_i$, the paths through $p_i$ whose points $x$ have $S(x)=S(p_i)-a\hbar$ for some $a\ge0$; these run into the critical points. The flow presses $\Gamma$ onto the thimbles,

$$\Gamma=n_1J_1+n_2J_2+\cdots+n_nJ_n,\tag{1}$$

where $n_i$ is the number of times $\Gamma$ crosses $K_i$, counted with signs [@witten2011]. So $I(\hbar)$ is the sum of the integrals over the thimbles, $n_i$ times each. The same flow deforms the domains of lattice path integrals towards thimbles, to tame their oscillation [@alexandru2022].

The Stokes phenomenon

The integral over each thimble has an asymptotic expansion as $\hbar\to0$ in a fixed direction. Near $p_i$, $S(x)=S(p_i)+\frac12S''(p_i)(x-p_i)^2+\cdots$. The quadratic part, integrated along the line $L_i$ that touches $J_i$ at $p_i$ (where $S''(p_i)(x-p_i)^2/\hbar$ is real and positive), gives the Gaussian integral

$$\int_{L_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S''(p_i)(x-p_i)^2/2\hbar}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar}{S''(p_i)}},$$

and the higher terms of $S$ give corrections in powers of $\hbar$:

$$\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx\;\sim\;\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar}{S''(p_i)}}\;\bigl(1+a^{(i)}_1\hbar+a^{(i)}_2\hbar^2+\cdots\bigr).\tag{2}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx\;&\sim\;\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar}{S''(p_i)}}\\&\quad\times\bigl(1+a^{(i)}_1\hbar+a^{(i)}_2\hbar^2+\cdots\bigr).\end{aligned}\tag{2}$$

The series in brackets, the series of $J_i$, diverges for every $\hbar\ne0$, so it describes the integral only as $\hbar\to0$. By $(1)$, $I(\hbar)$ is a sum of integrals over the thimbles $J_i$, each described by its divergent series.

The thimble $J_i$ jumps when the ray $S(p_i)+\hbar\,[0,\infty)$ runs into another critical value $S(p_j)$, that is, when $\arg\hbar=\arg\bigl(S(p_j)-S(p_i)\bigr)$: this ray in the plane of $\hbar$ is a Stokes ray of $J_i$. There $J_i$ runs into $p_j$, and as $\arg\hbar$ grows, $J_i$ becomes $J_i\pm J_j$: the Picard–Lefschetz formula [@pham1983]. The integral $I(\hbar)$ over the fixed path $\Gamma$ does not jump, but the coefficients in $(1)$ do: $n_j$ changes by $\mp n_i$, and a term $\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_j)/\hbar}$ enters the expansion of $I(\hbar)$ or leaves it. This is the Stokes phenomenon, which Stokes found in Airy’s integral [@stokes1864].

Turning $\hbar$ once round $0$ moves each valley on to the next, and $\Gamma$ moves with the valleys: $I(\hbar)$ is not a single-valued function of $\hbar$, and after a turn the coefficients $n_i$ in $(1)$ have changed. After $n+1$ turns each valley is back in its place, and so is $I(\hbar)$.

The Airy equation

The integrals $I(\hbar)$, one for each path $\Gamma$, are the solutions of a linear differential equation, and the Stokes phenomenon is a property of its solutions. This holds for every $S$, but for simplicity we treat Airy’s integral.

Airy’s integral has $S(x)=\frac13x^3-x$. Change the variables to

$$t=-\hbar^{-1/3}\,x,\qquad z=\hbar^{-2/3}:$$

the exponent becomes $-S(x)/\hbar=\frac13t^3-zt$, and

$$\begin{aligned}I(\hbar)=\int_\Gamma\mathrm{e}^{-S(x)/\hbar}\,dx&=-\hbar^{1/3}\,u(z),&u(z)&=\int_C\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt,\end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned}I(\hbar)=\int_\Gamma\mathrm{e}^{-S(x)/\hbar}\,dx&=-\hbar^{1/3}\,u(z),\\u(z)&=\int_C\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt,\end{aligned}$$

where $C$ is the image of $\Gamma$ under this change of variable: a path that comes in along one valley of $\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3}$ and goes out along another. Every such $u$ solves the Airy equation $u''=zu$, since

$$u''(z)-z\,u(z)=\int_C\bigl(t^2-z\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt=\int_C\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt=0,$$
$$\begin{aligned}u''(z)-z\,u(z)&=\int_C\bigl(t^2-z\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt\\&=\int_C\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt=0,\end{aligned}$$

the integrand vanishing at both ends of $C$. The three paths between the three valleys add up to $0$, and any two of them give two independent solutions, so every solution of the Airy equation is such an integral. For $\hbar>0$ and the path $\Gamma$ the page opens with, from the valley at angle $\frac{2\pi}3$ down to the one at $\frac{4\pi}3$, the path $C$ runs from $\infty\,\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/3}$ to $\infty\,\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}$, and $u=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{Ai}$, where

$$\mathrm{Ai}(z)=\frac1{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\infty\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/3}}^{\infty\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}}\mathrm{e}^{t^3/3-zt}\,dt$$

is Airy’s function [@dlmf]; so $I(\hbar)=-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\hbar^{1/3}\,\mathrm{Ai}\bigl(\hbar^{-2/3}\bigr)$.

With $\xi=\frac23z^{3/2}=\frac2{3\hbar}$, the exponentials $\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}$ of the two thimbles are $\mathrm{e}^{\mp\xi}$, since $S(\mp1)=\pm\frac23$, so by $(2)$ the integrals over $J_1$ and $J_2$ have the expansions

$$\begin{aligned}\int_{J_1}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx&\sim\pm2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\hbar^{1/3}\,w_1(z),&w_1(z)&=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{2\sqrt\pi\,z^{1/4}}\Bigl(1-\frac{5}{72\,\xi}+\cdots\Bigr),\\\int_{J_2}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx&\sim\pm2\pi\,\hbar^{1/3}\,w_2(z),&w_2(z)&=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\xi}}{2\sqrt\pi\,z^{1/4}}\Bigl(1+\frac{5}{72\,\xi}+\cdots\Bigr),\end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\int_{J_1}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx&\sim\pm2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\hbar^{1/3}\,w_1(z),\\w_1(z)&=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{2\sqrt\pi\,z^{1/4}}\Bigl(1-\frac{5}{72\,\xi}+\cdots\Bigr),\\\int_{J_2}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx&\sim\pm2\pi\,\hbar^{1/3}\,w_2(z),\\w_2(z)&=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\xi}}{2\sqrt\pi\,z^{1/4}}\Bigl(1+\frac{5}{72\,\xi}+\cdots\Bigr),\end{aligned}$$

the signs depending on the orientations of the thimbles. The functions $w_1$ and $w_2$ are the two formal solutions of the Airy equation that the WKB method gives as $z\to\infty$: each an exponential times a divergent series.

A solution is an integral over a fixed path, and an entire function of $z$; what changes is how it is expanded in $w_1$ and $w_2$. As $z\to\infty$,

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Ai}(z)&\sim w_1(z)&&\text{for }{-\tfrac{2\pi}3}<\arg z<\tfrac{2\pi}3,\\\mathrm{Ai}(z)&\sim w_1(z)\pm\mathrm{i}\,w_2(z)&&\text{for }\tfrac{2\pi}3<\pm\arg z\le\pi.\end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned}&\mathrm{Ai}(z)\sim w_1(z)\\&\qquad\text{for }{-\tfrac{2\pi}3}<\arg z<\tfrac{2\pi}3,\\&\mathrm{Ai}(z)\sim w_1(z)\pm\mathrm{i}\,w_2(z)\\&\qquad\text{for }\tfrac{2\pi}3<\pm\arg z\le\pi.\end{aligned}$$

The rays $\arg z=\pm\frac{2\pi}3$ are the Stokes rays $\arg\hbar=\mp\pi$ of $J_1$, since $\arg z=-\frac23\arg\hbar$.

On the real line the result is the chart below: $\mathrm{Ai}(z)$ is one decaying exponential, $w_1$, for $z>0$, and two oscillating ones, $w_1\pm\mathrm{i}\,w_2$, for $z<0$. This change of form is what Stokes found in Airy’s integral [@stokes1864].

The Borel plane

The Borel transform turns the divergent series of $J_i$ in $(2)$ into a convergent one that describes it. It divides the coefficients by $(\frac12)_k=\frac12\cdot\frac32\cdots\bigl(k-\frac12\bigr)=\Gamma(k+\frac12)/\sqrt\pi$, which grow factorially, as fast as $k!$ up to a power of $k$ (about $(k-1)!\sqrt{k/\pi}$):

$$\beta_i(\zeta)=1+\frac{a^{(i)}_1}{(\frac12)_1}\,\zeta+\frac{a^{(i)}_2}{(\frac12)_2}\,\zeta^2+\cdots.\tag{3}$$

Note that many texts divide by $k!$ instead, which works in the same way; we use $(\frac12)_k$ so that the transform will come straight out of the integral below.

Unlike the series of $J_i$, the series $(3)$ converges near $\zeta=0$, and substituting $\zeta=S(x)-S(p_i)$ in $\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx$ gives that integral as a Laplace integral of $\beta_i$ along the ray of $\hbar$:

$$\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx=\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2}{S''(p_i)}}\int_0^{\infty\cdot\hbar}\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta/\hbar}\,\beta_i(\zeta)\,\frac{d\zeta}{\sqrt\zeta}.\tag{4}$$
$$\begin{aligned}\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx&=\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{2}{S''(p_i)}}\\&\quad\times\int_0^{\infty\cdot\hbar}\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta/\hbar}\,\beta_i(\zeta)\,\frac{d\zeta}{\sqrt\zeta}.\end{aligned}\tag{4}$$

Integrated term by term, with

$$\int_0^\infty\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta/\hbar}\,\zeta^{k-1/2}\,d\zeta=\sqrt\pi\,(\tfrac12)_k\,\hbar^{k+1/2},$$

the Laplace integral gives back the series $(2)$: the factors $(\frac12)_k$ come back.

The Borel plane, the plane of $\zeta=S(x)-S(p_i)$, is the plane of values of $S$ with $S(p_i)$ moved to $0$, and the integrand of $(4)$ decays along the ray over the length $|\hbar|$. Along each ray from $0$, $\beta_i$ continues analytically until the ray meets one of the points $A_{ij}=S(p_j)-S(p_i)$ for which $J_i$ runs into $p_j$ at $\arg\hbar=\arg A_{ij}$. There it has a singularity like $(\zeta-A_{ij})^{-1/2}$, and beyond it the values on the two sides of the ray differ (the red rays in the picture). So the Laplace integral $(4)$ jumps when the ray of $\hbar$ crosses a singularity, exactly as the thimble does [@ecalle1981; @aniceto2019].

Resurgence

The series of $J_i$ in $(2)$ diverges for every $\hbar\ne0$, but as an asymptotic expansion it is accurate as $\hbar\to0$ for each fixed number of terms. Its terms first fall and then grow rapidly, and so does the error of the sum of its first $k$ terms.

The coefficients $a^{(i)}_k$ of the series of $J_i$ come from Laplace’s method. Consider the Taylor expansion of $S$ about $p_i$, written as

$$S(p_i+y)=S(p_i)+s_2y^2\bigl(1+T(y)\bigr),\qquad s_2=\tfrac12S''(p_i),$$
$$\begin{aligned}S(p_i+y)&=S(p_i)+s_2y^2\bigl(1+T(y)\bigr),\\s_2&=\tfrac12S''(p_i),\end{aligned}$$

where the power series $T(y)=t_1y+t_2y^2+\cdots$ holds the terms of $S$ of degree three and more. Then

$$a^{(i)}_k=\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(2s_2)^k}\,[y^{2k}]\,\bigl(1+T(y)\bigr)^{-(2k+1)/2},$$

where $[y^{2k}]$ takes the coefficient of $y^{2k}$ in the power series after it. To see this, change the variable in $\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx$ from $x=p_i+y$ to $w$, with $w^2=(S(x)-S(p_i))/\hbar$, which is real and positive on $J_i$: the integrand becomes $\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\,\mathrm{e}^{-w^2}\,dx/dw$, and $w$ runs over the real line. Expand $dx/dw$ in powers of $w$ (Lagrange’s inversion formula gives its coefficients from those of $(1+T)^{-1/2}$), and integrate term by term against $\mathrm{e}^{-w^2}$: the odd powers give $0$, and the power $w^{2k}$ gives the term $a^{(i)}_k\hbar^k$ of $(2)$.

Divided by $(\frac12)_k$, the $a^{(i)}_k$ are the Taylor coefficients of $\beta_i$ in $(3)$, and the series $(3)$ converges only out to the singularity $A$ of $\beta_i$ nearest to $0$. So the $a^{(i)}_k$ grow like $(k-1)!/|A|^k$, and the terms $a^{(i)}_k\hbar^k$ are smallest at about $k=|A/\hbar|$. The optimal truncation is the sum of the terms before the smallest, the first $N\approx|A/\hbar|$ of them: it comes as close as the series can to $\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx$ divided by the factor in front of the series in $(2)$, within about $\mathrm{e}^{-|A/\hbar|}$ [@boyd1999].

Across a Stokes ray of $J_i$, where $J_i$ runs into $p_j$, $J_i$ becomes $J_i\pm J_j$, so $\int_{J_i}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx$ jumps by $\pm\int_{J_j}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx$. Divided by the factor in front of the series of $J_i$ in $(2)$, and expanded by $(2)$ for $J_j$, the jump is

$$\frac{\pm\int_{J_j}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx}{\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{2\pi\hbar/S''(p_i)}}\;\sim\;\kappa_{ij}\,\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}\bigl(1+a^{(j)}_1\hbar+a^{(j)}_2\hbar^2+\cdots\bigr),\tag{5}$$
$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{\pm\int_{J_j}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx}{\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{2\pi\hbar/S''(p_i)}}\\&\quad\sim\;\kappa_{ij}\,\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}\bigl(1+a^{(j)}_1\hbar+\cdots\bigr),\end{aligned}\tag{5}$$

where $A_{ij}=S(p_j)-S(p_i)$, and the Stokes constant $\kappa_{ij}=\pm\sqrt{S''(p_i)/S''(p_j)}$ carries the sign of the jump and the orientations of the thimbles. Cauchy’s formula for the Taylor coefficients of $\beta_i$, with its contour moved out onto the cuts of $\beta_i$, where $\beta_i$ jumps in this way, turns this into the large orders of the series of $J_i$:

$$a^{(i)}_k\;\approx\;\sum_j\frac{\kappa_{ij}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\Bigl(\frac{\Gamma(k)}{A_{ij}^{\,k}}+a^{(j)}_1\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{A_{ij}^{\,k-1}}+a^{(j)}_2\frac{\Gamma(k-2)}{A_{ij}^{\,k-2}}+\cdots\Bigr),\tag{6}$$
$$\begin{aligned}a^{(i)}_k\approx\sum_j\frac{\kappa_{ij}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\Bigl(&\frac{\Gamma(k)}{A_{ij}^{\,k}}+a^{(j)}_1\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{A_{ij}^{\,k-1}}\\&+a^{(j)}_2\frac{\Gamma(k-2)}{A_{ij}^{\,k-2}}+\cdots\Bigr),\end{aligned}\tag{6}$$

the sum over the critical points $p_j$ that $J_i$ runs into, and in the brackets over $l=0,1,\dots,k-1$. For large $k$ the term with $a^{(j)}_l$ is about $|A_{ij}/k|^l\,|a^{(j)}_l|$ times the first, so the coefficients up to $a^{(j)}_L$ of each of these series give $a^{(i)}_k$ to a relative accuracy of about $k^{-L-1}$, better the larger $k$.

So the late coefficients of the series of $J_i$ are determined by the early coefficients of the series of the thimbles $J_j$ that $J_i$ runs into: this is resurgence [@ecalle1981; @berry1991].

Smoothing the jump

Seen from the optimal truncation of the series of $J_i$, the Stokes phenomenon is not a jump but a smooth, rapid change across the Stokes ray, the narrower the smaller $\hbar$ [@berry1989].

Let $\hbar$ cross a Stokes ray of $J_i$, where $J_i$ runs into $p_j$, and let $J_i^-$ be the thimble $J_i$ as it is just before the ray, kept fixed as $\hbar$ crosses it; the integral over $J_i^-$ does not jump. Divide it by the factor in front of the series of $J_i$ in $(2)$, and take away the series of $J_i$ truncated optimally, after $N\approx|A_{ij}/\hbar|$ terms. What is left is the remainder

$$R_N(\hbar)=\frac{\int_{J_i^-}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx}{\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{2\pi\hbar/S''(p_i)}}-\bigl(1+a^{(i)}_1\hbar+\cdots+a^{(i)}_{N-1}\hbar^{N-1}\bigr).$$
$$\begin{aligned}R_N(\hbar)={}&\frac{\int_{J_i^-}\mathrm{e}^{-S/\hbar}\,dx}{\mathrm{e}^{-S(p_i)/\hbar}\sqrt{2\pi\hbar/S''(p_i)}}\\&-\bigl(1+a^{(i)}_1\hbar+\cdots+a^{(i)}_{N-1}\hbar^{N-1}\bigr).\end{aligned}$$

In the expansion by thimbles the jumping term $\kappa_{ij}\,\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}\bigl(1+a^{(j)}_1\hbar+\cdots\bigr)$ of $(5)$ enters all at once: before the ray $J_i^-$ is the thimble $J_i$, and after it $J_i^-$ is the new thimble $J_i$ less the jump, so $R_N$ goes from about $0$ to minus that term as soon as $\hbar$ crosses the ray. But $R_N$ itself changes smoothly. Berry found [@berry1989]

$$R_N(\hbar)\;\approx\;-\tfrac12\bigl(1+\operatorname{erf}\sigma\bigr)\,\kappa_{ij}\,\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}\bigl(1+a^{(j)}_1\hbar+a^{(j)}_2\hbar^2+\cdots\bigr),\tag{7}$$
$$\begin{aligned}R_N(\hbar)\approx{}&-\tfrac12\bigl(1+\operatorname{erf}\sigma\bigr)\\&\times\kappa_{ij}\,\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}\bigl(1+a^{(j)}_1\hbar+\cdots\bigr),\end{aligned}\tag{7}$$

where $\sigma=-\mathrm{Im}(A_{ij}/\hbar)\big/\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{Re}(A_{ij}/\hbar)}$, which is about $(\arg\hbar-\arg A_{ij})\sqrt{|A_{ij}/\hbar|/2}$. So in the remainder the coefficient of the jumping term rises from $0$, through $\frac12$ on the ray, to $1$, over an angle of about $\sqrt{2|\hbar/A_{ij}|}$ on either side of the ray. That is the difference the chart shows: the thimbles switch the exponential $\mathrm{e}^{-A_{ij}/\hbar}$ on at once, while the optimally truncated series switches it on smoothly, the more abruptly the smaller $\hbar$.
