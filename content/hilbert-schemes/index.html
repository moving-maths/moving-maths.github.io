The Hilbert scheme $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ is the space of zero-dimensional subschemes of length $n$ in the plane: sets of $n$ distinct points, and their limits as points collide. Each is cut out by an ideal $I\subset\CC[x,y]$ with $\dim\CC[x,y]/I=n$, so $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ is also the space of these ideals. The figures below show its real points.

Points

The ideal and its staircase

The ideal $I$ consists of the polynomials $f$ that vanish at every chosen point: $f(p)=0$. At a point of multiplicity $k$ along a curve $\gamma$, with $\gamma(0)=p$, $f$ vanishes to order $k$ along the curve: $f(\gamma(s))=O(s^k)$. At a double point with direction $v$, this means $f(p)=\partial_vf(p)=0$.

The monomials $x^ay^b$ are ordered by their weight $w_1a+w_2b$, ties broken by the power of $y$, for the weight $w$ on the dial beside the staircase. A monomial is kept if no polynomial in $I$ has it as its leading term, its term of greatest weight. Otherwise $I$ contains a polynomial whose other terms are kept monomials:

$$x^ay^b-\sum_{s\ \text{kept}}c_s\,s\ \in\ I.$$

If $x^ay^b$ is not a multiple of an earlier leading term, this polynomial is a generator of $I$. The kept monomials are not in $I$. There are $n$ of them, they form the staircase, and they are a basis of $\CC[x,y]/I$. The generators, one at each corner of the staircase, form the reduced Gröbner basis of $I$ for this order [@cox2015, Ch. 2].

Multiplication by $x$ and by $y$ on $\CC[x,y]/I$, in the basis of the staircase, are two commuting $n\times n$ matrices $X$ and $Y$. In the figure above, $x$ moves a box one step right and $y$ one step up; a monomial outside the staircase is a combination of boxes in $\CC[x,y]/I$. The joint eigenvalues of $X$ and $Y$ are the points, a point of multiplicity $k$ counted $k$ times. The vector $1$ is cyclic: the matrices applied to it span $\CC^n$. Two commuting $n\times n$ matrices with a cyclic vector, up to a change of basis, are a point of $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ [@nakajima1999, Ch. 1].

Colliding points

When points collide, their ideal has a limit. As a point comes to another point $p$ along the direction $v$, their ideal converges to $(\ell_v)+\mathfrak m_p^2$, where $\ell_v$ is the equation of the line through $p$ along $v$ and $\mathfrak m_p$ is the ideal of $p$. This is the double point at $p$ with direction $v$. The double points at one place form a projective line $\PP^1$, and $\Hilb^2(\CC^2)$ is $\Sym^2(\CC^2)$ with its diagonal blown up.

Three points that come together along a curve $y=cx^2+\dots$ through the origin converge to the curvilinear triple point

$$\bigl(y-cx^2,\ x^3\bigr).$$

Three points that come from three directions at once converge to $\mathfrak m_p^2$. The disc above holds all the real triple points at one place: $\mathfrak m_p^2$ at its centre, and the triple points along straight lines on its rim, where opposite points are the same.

The torus and its fixed points

The torus $T=(\CC^\times)^2$ acts on the plane by scaling the coordinates, and so acts on $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$. For the weight $w=(w_1,w_2)$, the points move to the origin along $t\mapsto(t^{w_1}x,\,t^{w_2}y)$ as $t$ goes from $1$ to $0$. Their ideal $I_t$ converges to the initial ideal of $I$, generated by the terms of greatest weight of the generators [@eisenbud1995, §15.8]:

$$\lim_{t\to0}I_t=\init_w(I).$$

The ticks on the dial of $w$ are the weights at which two terms of a generator weigh the same. For $w$ between two ticks, $\init_w(I)$ is the monomial ideal of the staircase; for $w$ on a tick, it is not monomial.

The points of $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ fixed by $T$ are the monomial ideals with $n$ monomials outside them, one for each partition of $n$; the strip under the picture shows them. The points that flow to the same fixed point form a cell [@bialynicki-birula1973], so the Euler characteristic of $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ is the number of partitions of $n$:

$$\sum_{n\ge0}\chi\bigl(\Hilb^n(\CC^2)\bigr)\,q^n=\prod_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{1-q^k}.$$

Its Betti numbers count the partitions by their number of rows, as the strip groups them: $b_{2i}$ is the number of partitions of $n$ into $n-i$ rows [@ellingsrud1987].

Tangent spaces at the fixed points

The tangent space to $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ at the fixed point $I_\lambda$ is $\Hom(I_\lambda,\CC[x,y]/I_\lambda)$. It splits into weight spaces for $T$: a homomorphism of weight $(d_1,d_2)$ takes each monomial $x^iy^j$ of $I_\lambda$ to a multiple of $x^{i+d_1}y^{j+d_2}$. Each box $s$ of the diagram of $\lambda$ gives two weights [@ellingsrud1987; @nakajima1999, Ch. 5; @haiman1998]. Its arm $a(s)$ is the number of boxes to its right, and its leg $l(s)$ the number of boxes above it, both grey in the figure; its weights are

$$\bigl(-a(s)-1,\ l(s)\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl(a(s),\ -l(s)-1\bigr)$$

In the figure each weight is an arrow from a monomial outside the diagram to one inside. So the tangent space has dimension $2n$ at every fixed point. Every point flows to a fixed point, and the dimension of the tangent space can only grow in a limit, so it is $2n$ everywhere: $\Hilb^n(\CC^2)$ is smooth, of dimension $2n$ [@fogarty1968].
