An irreducible finite-dimensional representation of a simple Lie algebra is determined by its highest weight $\lambda$, and denoted $V(\lambda)$. Explore its weights and their multiplicities, tensor products, Verma modules, and the representations of affine Lie algebras.

Weights and multiplicities

The irreducible finite-dimensional representations of a simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ are the $V(\lambda)$ with highest weight $\lambda$, one for each dominant integral weight $\lambda$. $V(\lambda)$ is the sum of its weight spaces $V(\lambda)_\mu$, on which a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ acts by the weight $\mu$, and the multiplicity of $\mu$ is $m(\mu)=\dim V(\lambda)_\mu$. The weights are the points of $\lambda+Q$, $Q$ the root lattice, in the convex hull of the orbit $W\lambda$ of the Weyl group, and $W$ permutes them, preserving their multiplicities [@humphreys1972, §21].

So the multiplicities of the dominant weights determine all the others, and Freudenthal’s formula computes them one after another, starting from $m(\lambda)=1$ [@humphreys1972, §22.3]:

$$m(\mu)=\frac{2\sum_{\alpha>0}\sum_{k\ge1}(\mu+k\alpha,\alpha)\,m(\mu+k\alpha)}{\lvert\lambda+\rho\rvert^2-\lvert\mu+\rho\rvert^2},$$

where $\rho$ is half the sum of the positive roots. The multiplicities add up to the dimension, given by Weyl’s formula as a product over the positive roots [@humphreys1972, §24]:

$$\dim V(\lambda)=\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}.$$

For $\mathfrak{sl}_n$, with $\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i\varpi_i$, the $\lambda_i$ being the numbers on the nodes of its Dynkin diagram, $\lambda$ corresponds to the partition with $\lambda_i+\dots+\lambda_{n-1}$ boxes in row $i$. The multiplicity of $\mu$ is the number of semistandard tableaux of this shape and weight $\mu$, where a tableau with $c_j$ entries equal to $j$ has weight $\sum_i(c_i-c_{i+1})\varpi_i$ [@fulton1991, Lecture 15].

Hexagons and triangles

For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and $\lambda=a\varpi_1+b\varpi_2$, the weights lie on $\min(a,b)$ nested hexagons and, inside them, on triangles. The multiplicity is $1$ on the outer hexagon, one more on each hexagon further in, and $\min(a,b)+1$ on every triangle [@fulton1991, Lecture 13].

The relief

The relief draws the multiplicities of $V(\lambda)$, in rank two, as the heights of columns, one on each weight. Its shape has a limit as $\lambda$ is scaled up [@heckman1982]. Let $d$ be the number of positive roots not orthogonal to $\lambda$, minus the rank. Shrunk by $k$ across and by $k^d$ in height, the relief of $V(k\lambda)$ tends to the graph of a function on the convex hull of $W\lambda$ that is a polynomial of degree $d$ on each of finitely many pieces: the density of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of the coadjoint orbit through $\lambda$.

For $\lambda$ off the walls of the dominant chamber, $d$ is $1$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_3$, $2$ for $\mathfrak{so}_5$ and $4$ for $\mathfrak g_2$. For $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ the limit rises linearly from the hexagon to the triangle and is flat on the triangle, as the multiplicities do.

Tensor products

The tensor product of two irreducible representations is a sum of irreducible ones,

$$V(\lambda)\otimes V(\nu)=\bigoplus_\kappa n_\kappa\,V(\kappa),$$

and the Brauer–Klimyk rule computes the multiplicities $n_\kappa$ from the weights of $V(\nu)$ [@humphreys1972, §24]. Each weight $\mu$ of $V(\nu)$, of multiplicity $m_\nu(\mu)$, is placed at $\lambda+\mu$. A point in the dominant chamber adds $m_\nu(\mu)$ copies of $V(\lambda+\mu)$. A point outside it is moved into the chamber by the dot action $w\cdot x=w(x+\rho)-\rho$, and adds $\varepsilon(w)\,m_\nu(\mu)$ copies of $V\bigl(w\cdot(\lambda+\mu)\bigr)$, where $\varepsilon(w)=\det w=\pm1$; a point on a mirror through $-\rho$ adds nothing. In all,

$$n_\kappa=\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)\,m_\nu(w\cdot\kappa-\lambda).$$

When $\lambda$ lies so far inside the chamber that every $\lambda+\mu$ is dominant, nothing moves, and $V(\lambda)\otimes V(\nu)=\bigoplus_\mu m_\nu(\mu)\,V(\lambda+\mu)$.

Verma modules

The Verma module $M(\lambda)$, for any weight $\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$, is generated by a vector of weight $\lambda$ killed by the positive root vectors, with no other relations:

$$M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak b)}\CC_\lambda.$$

Its weights are the $\lambda-\beta$ with $\beta$ a sum of positive roots, and the multiplicity of $\lambda-\beta$ is Kostant’s partition function $P(\beta)$, the number of ways of writing $\beta$ as such a sum [@humphreys2008, Ch. 1]. $M(\lambda)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$, which is $V(\lambda)$ when $\lambda$ is dominant integral.

The dot action $w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$ governs the submodules. If $n=\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ is a positive integer for a positive root $\alpha$, then $M(\lambda)$ contains $M(s_\alpha\cdot\lambda)$, whose highest weight is $\lambda-n\alpha$; if there is no such root, $M(\lambda)$ is simple [@humphreys2008, Ch. 4].

The character of $L(\lambda)$ is given by Kazhdan–Lusztig theory [@humphreys2008, Ch. 7–8]. The roots $\alpha$ with $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ an integer form a root system, whose Weyl group $W_\lambda$ acts by the dot action. Let $\lambda_0$ be the lowest weight in the orbit $W_\lambda\cdot\lambda$, and $x$ the shortest element of $W_\lambda$ that takes $\lambda_0$ to $\lambda$. Then

$$\operatorname{ch}L(\lambda)=\sum_{y\le x}(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(y)}\,P_{y,x}(1)\,\operatorname{ch}M(y\cdot\lambda_0),$$

where $P_{y,x}$ are the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of $W_\lambda$ and $\le$ is its Bruhat order. In rank one and two, $W_\lambda$ is a dihedral group or smaller, and every $P_{y,x}$ is $1$.

Affine Lie algebras

The affine Lie algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak g}$ is the central extension of the loop algebra $\mathfrak g\otimes\CC[t,t^{-1}]$ by a central element $K$, with a derivation added [@kac1990, Ch. 7]. Its Dynkin diagram is that of $\mathfrak g$ with one more node, $0$, for the simple root $\alpha_0=\delta-\theta$, where $\theta$ is the highest root of $\mathfrak g$ and $\delta$ the smallest positive imaginary root.

A weight $\Lambda$ is given, up to a multiple of $\delta$, by its labels $\lambda_i=\langle\Lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$, one for each node: $\Lambda=\sum_{i=0}^r\lambda_i\Lambda_i$, with $\Lambda_i$ the fundamental weights. The labels are the numbers on the Dynkin diagram next to the picture, where they can be changed.

The irreducible representation $L(\Lambda)$ with highest weight $\Lambda$ is generated by a vector of weight $\Lambda$ killed by the positive root vectors. There is one for each $\Lambda$ [@kac1990, Ch. 9], and $K$ acts on it as the scalar

$$k=\sum_{i=0}^r a_i^\vee\lambda_i,$$

the level, where $a_0^\vee=1$ and $\theta^\vee=\sum_{i\ge1}a_i^\vee\alpha_i^\vee$.

The representation $L(\Lambda)$ is integrable when every $e_i$ and $f_i$ acts locally nilpotently, each vector killed by a power of it. Then $L(\Lambda)$ is a sum of finite-dimensional representations of the copy of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ spanned by $e_i$, $f_i$ and $\alpha_i^\vee$, for each $i$. This is so exactly when $\Lambda$ is dominant integral, every $\lambda_i$ a non-negative integer [@kac1990, Ch. 10], and then the level is a non-negative integer too; these are the $\Lambda$ the picture allows with the Any weight option off.

The weights of an integrable $L(\Lambda)$ are the conjugates, under the affine Weyl group, of the dominant weights below $\Lambda$, and Freudenthal’s formula gives their multiplicities, with the imaginary roots $n\delta$ counted $r$ times [@kac1990, Ch. 11]. Each weight lies on a string $\mu-n\delta$, $n\ge0$, below a maximal weight $\mu$ [@kac1990, Ch. 12], and the picture draws $\mu-n\delta$ at $n$ steps below $\mu$. Every weight $\mu$ has $(\mu|\mu)\le(\Lambda|\Lambda)$, so the weights lie in a paraboloid [@kac1990, Ch. 11].

For $\mathfrak g$ simply laced, here $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $\mathfrak{sl}_3$, the multiplicity of $\mu-n\delta$ in the basic representation $L(\Lambda_0)$, for $\mu$ in $W\Lambda_0$, is the number $p_r(n)$ of partitions of $n$ into parts of $r$ colours [@frenkel1980]:

$$\sum_{n\ge0}p_r(n)\,q^n=\prod_{m\ge1}\frac{1}{(1-q^m)^r}.$$

Verma modules

The Verma module $M(\Lambda)$, for any weight $\Lambda$, is generated by a vector of weight $\Lambda$ killed by the positive root vectors of $\widehat{\mathfrak g}$, with no other relations:

$$M(\Lambda)=U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})\otimes_{U(\widehat{\mathfrak b})}\CC_\Lambda.$$

Its weights are the $\Lambda-\beta$ with $\beta$ a sum of positive roots, and the multiplicity of $\Lambda-\beta$ is the number of ways of writing $\beta$ as such a sum, each imaginary root $n\delta$ counted $r$ times [@kac1990, Ch. 9]. $M(\Lambda)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(\Lambda)$, the integrable one above when $\Lambda$ is dominant integral.

With the dot action $w\cdot\Lambda=w(\Lambda+\rho)-\rho$ of the affine Weyl group: if $n=\langle\Lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ is a positive integer for a positive real root $\alpha$, then $L(s_\alpha\cdot\Lambda)$, whose highest weight is $\Lambda-n\alpha$, is a subquotient of $M(\Lambda)$. When there is no such root, $L(\Lambda)$ is $M(\Lambda)$ itself, unless the level is critical [@kac1979]: $k=-h^\vee$, where $h^\vee=\sum_{i=0}^r a_i^\vee$ is the dual Coxeter number, and then $(\Lambda+\rho\mid\delta)=0$. For $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ the critical level is $-2$. For $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_3$ the critical level is $-3$. For $\widehat{\mathfrak{so}}_5$ the critical level is $-3$. For $\widehat{\mathfrak g}_2$ the critical level is $-4$.

For $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ off the critical level the integral Weyl group is dihedral, finite or infinite, its Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials are all $1$, and $\operatorname{ch}L(\Lambda)$ is an alternating sum of characters of Verma modules [@kashiwara1995]. Otherwise $L(\Lambda)$ is built from its definition, one weight space after another: a vector below $\Lambda$ is zero in $L(\Lambda)$ exactly when every $e_j$ sends it to zero.
