The configuration spaces of a space $X$: the space of $k$-tuples of distinct points in $X$, or of $k$-element subsets when the points are unlabelled.

Why there’s no picture

Three points in the plane

The space $\Conf_3(\RR^2)$ of three points in the plane is homotopy equivalent to the $3$-sphere minus three circles. With the plane as $\CC$, the points are a vector $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ of $\CC^3$. Moving them until their centre is $0$ puts them in the plane $x_1+x_2+x_3=0$, a copy of $\CC^2$, and scaling them to length $1$ puts them on its unit sphere $S^3$; neither changes the homotopy type. The collision $x_i=x_j$ is a complex line in that plane, which meets $S^3$ in a great circle $C_{ij}$:

$$\Conf_3(\RR^2)\simeq S^3\setminus(C_{12}\cup C_{13}\cup C_{23}).$$

The three circles are fibres of the Hopf fibration $S^3\to\CC P^1$, and any two of them are linked once; stereographic projection draws them as circles in space. How often a closed path goes round $C_{ij}$, its linking number with $C_{ij}$, is how often $x_j$ goes round $x_i$, the winding number of $x_j-x_i$ round $0$. The three numbers give the class of the path in $H_1\bigl(\Conf_3(\RR^2)\bigr)=\ZZ^3$.

Three unordered points, centred, are the roots of a cubic $z^3+az+b$. Scaling the roots by $t$ takes $(a,b)$ to $(t^2a,t^3b)$, so up to scale these cubics form $S^3$ again. Two roots coincide where $4a^3+27b^2=0$, which meets $S^3$ in a trefoil knot $K$:

$$\UConf_3(\RR^2)\simeq S^3\setminus K.$$

So the group of the trefoil, the fundamental group of its complement, is the braid group $B_3$. A swap of two points is a loop once round $K$.

Braids

A braid of $k$ strands in $X$ is made of $k$ disjoint curves in $X\times[0,1]$, each meeting every level $X\times\{t\}$ once. Points moving in $X$ trace a braid, a strand for each point: a path in $\Conf_k(X)$ is a braid, and a loop, which brings each point back to its place, is a pure braid; a loop in $\UConf_k(X)$ may permute the points. Up to deformation the loops form the fundamental groups

$$\begin{gathered}\pi_1\Conf_k(X)=P_k(X),\\ \pi_1\UConf_k(X)=B_k(X),\end{gathered}$$

the pure braid group and the braid group of $X$. For the plane they are Artin’s braid groups. Half a turn of two points about each other swaps them, a braid that is not pure. A whole turn, one point once round the other, is a pure braid.

Forgetting the last point is a fibration $\Conf_k(X)\to\Conf_{k-1}(X)$ whose fibre is $X$ minus $k-1$ points [@fadell1962]. The fibres are aspherical, so when $X$ is aspherical, so is $\Conf_k(X)$, and its homotopy type is determined by its braid group. That is so for every space here but the sphere and the projective plane, for which $\pi_3\Conf_k(X)\cong\pi_3(X)\cong\ZZ$.

With the points in a row, the fibrations give generators [@fadell1962]: $P_k(X)$ is generated by the loops of each $x_j$ once round each $x_i$ with $i For the plane these are Artin’s generators [@artin1947]. The swaps of neighbours in the row lift the transpositions that generate the permutations, so with the loops of $x_1$ they generate $B_k(X)$.

Collisions

The collisions divide $X^k$ into strata, one for each way the points can coincide: for ordered points a partition of $\{1,\dots,k\}$ into blocks of points at one place, and for unordered points, in $\Sym^k(X)$, a partition of $k$, the multiplicities. A stratum of $\ell$ blocks is a copy of $\Conf_\ell(X)$, for unordered points divided by the permutations of the points of equal multiplicity. It has codimension $(k-\ell)\dim X$, and its closure also contains the strata got from it by merging blocks, below it in the figure.

Cohomology

The Betti numbers $b_i$ of a space are the dimensions of its rational cohomology groups $H^i$, and its Poincaré polynomial is $\sum_ib_it^i$.

For $k$ ordered points in the plane the Poincaré polynomial is [@arnold1969]

$$\sum_i b_i\bigl(\Conf_k(\RR^2)\bigr)\,t^i=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(1+jt).$$

The factor $1+jt$ is the Poincaré polynomial of the plane minus $j$ points, the fibre of the fibration $\Conf_{j+1}(\RR^2)\to\Conf_j(\RR^2)$ that forgets $x_{j+1}$ [@fadell1962]: the Betti numbers are those of the product of the fibres. The cohomology ring is generated by classes $\omega_{ij}$ of degree $1$, where $\omega_{ij}$ of a loop is the number of times $x_j$ goes round $x_i$.
